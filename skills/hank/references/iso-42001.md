# ISO/IEC 42001:2023: Artificial Intelligence Management System

**Citation:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (first edition, published December 2023). The first international standard for an AI Management System (AIMS). Companion standards: ISO/IEC 22989:2022 (AI concepts + terminology), ISO/IEC 23894:2023 (AI risk management).

**Enforcement:** Voluntary international standard. Certification offered by accredited bodies (A-LIGN was first to market). Becoming the de-facto AI governance benchmark and the most-cited pre-EU-AI-Act compliance signal.

---

## Scope: who it applies to

Any organisation that **provides, produces, or uses AI systems**. ISO 42001 defines three roles:

- **AI provider**: provides products/services that use AI systems (includes AI platform providers + AI product/service providers)
- **AI producer**: designs, develops, tests, deploys products/services using AI systems (model designers, implementers, verifiers)
- **AI user**: uses an AI product/service directly or provides it to AI users

Most organisations occupy multiple roles. A small AI agency, for example, is often a provider (its own AI product), a producer (building AI features for clients) and a user (every AI tool in its own stack) at once.

## Structure: same Annex A pattern as ISO 27001

**Clauses 4–10 = mandatory AIMS requirements** (mirrors ISO 27001 structure to enable integrated management systems):

- **Clause 4: Context.** Internal + external issues, interested parties, AIMS scope, AI system roles.
- **Clause 5: Leadership.** Top management commitment, AI policy, roles + responsibilities, accountability.
- **Clause 6: Planning.** AI risk assessment, AI risk treatment, AI impact assessment, objectives.
- **Clause 7: Support.** Resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information.
- **Clause 8: Operation.** AI system lifecycle processes, data management, third-party + customer relationships.
- **Clause 9: Performance evaluation.** Monitoring, measurement, internal audit, management review.
- **Clause 10: Improvement.** Nonconformity, corrective action, continual improvement.

**Annex A = 38 controls grouped into 9 objectives** (lighter than ISO 27001's 93):

| Objective | Controls | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| A.2 Policies related to AI | 2 | AI policy, alignment with org policies |
| A.3 Internal organisation | 3 | Roles + responsibilities, reporting of concerns |
| A.4 Resources for AI systems | 6 | Resources docs, data resources, tooling resources, system + computing resources, human resources, resource documentation |
| A.5 Assessing impacts of AI systems | 5 | Process, documentation, impact on individuals + groups + society, impact assessment in lifecycle |
| A.6 AI system lifecycle | 7 | Mgmt guidance for development, objectives, responsible AI development, design + dev processes, verification + validation, deployment, operation + monitoring, tech docs, event logging |
| A.7 Data for AI systems | 5 | Data quality, data lineage, data prep, data management |
| A.8 Information for interested parties | 4 | System docs + info, external reporting, communication of incidents, info for interested parties |
| A.9 Use of AI systems | 3 | Processes for responsible use, objectives, intended use |
| A.10 Third-party + customer relationships | 3 | Allocation of responsibilities, suppliers, customers |

Annex B provides implementation guidance per control. Annex C catalogues AI-related organisational objectives + risk sources. Annex D covers domain/sector-specific standards.

## What's distinct from ISO 27001

ISO 27001 protects **information**. ISO 42001 governs **AI systems**: a different object with different failure modes.

| Concern | ISO 27001 covers | ISO 42001 covers |
|---|---|---|
| Confidentiality of customer data | ✅ | Inherits from 27001 |
| Model bias + fairness | n/a | ✅ |
| Training data quality + lineage | Partial (info asset) | ✅ (data for AI) |
| AI system lifecycle (design → retire) | n/a | ✅ |
| AI impact on individuals / society | n/a | ✅ |
| Transparency + explainability | n/a | ✅ |
| Human oversight of AI decisions | n/a | ✅ |
| Continuous monitoring of model behaviour | Partial (monitoring) | ✅ (AI-specific) |
| Prompt injection / model exploitation | n/a (Hank's territory) | Partial (operational controls) |

**The two standards are designed to integrate.** A-LIGN and other auditors offer joint 27001+42001 assessments: share the management-system backbone, add the AI-specific controls.

## AI risk management + AI impact assessment

ISO 42001 mandates **two assessments** that don't exist in ISO 27001:

### AI risk assessment (Clause 6.1.2)

Identifies + analyses risks to the *organisation* from AI systems. Sources include:
- Technical risks (model failure, drift, adversarial attack)
- Operational risks (data quality, dependency on third-party models)
- Reputational risks (biased output reaching customers)
- Compliance risks (EU AI Act, GDPR, sector-specific)
- Financial risks (token cost overruns, lock-in)

Methodology comes from companion standard **ISO/IEC 23894:2023**: applies ISO 31000 risk management principles to AI.

### AI system impact assessment (Annex A.5)

Identifies + analyses impacts on *individuals, groups, society*. Required for any AI system the organisation provides/produces. Covers:
- Health, safety, fundamental rights
- Privacy implications
- Bias + discrimination
- Environmental impact
- Economic + social consequences

This assessment is the closest thing in the global AI governance landscape to the EU AI Act's **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA, Art. 27)**: an ISO 42001 impact assessment covers much of the FRIA's evidence trail.

## ISO 42001 + EU AI Act

ISO 42001 is **not a harmonised standard** under the EU AI Act, so certifying to it does not give presumption of conformity. The European QMS standard written for AI Act Art. 17 is **EN 18286:2026** (published July 2026), not ISO 42001. See `eu-ai-act.md` for the standards status.

In practice:
- Certifying to ISO 42001 produces much of the management-system evidence the AI Act asks of high-risk providers, though it is not a substitute for EN 18286 or conformity assessment
- ISO 42001's AI impact assessment is the closest existing analogue to the AI Act's Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (Art. 27)
- ISO 42001 + ISO 27001 + ISO 23894 together form the most defensible audit-ready stack for EU enterprise AI buyers in 2026
- Early certifiers (Synthesia was among the first, in 2024) are using the standard for procurement differentiation

## ISO 42001 + NIST AI RMF

NIST AI RMF is voluntary US risk management; ISO 42001 is voluntary international management system. They're complementary, not competitive.

- **NIST AI RMF** = how to think about AI risk (Govern / Map / Measure / Manage)
- **ISO 42001** = how to operationalise AI governance as a management system

Many orgs use NIST AI RMF as the risk methodology *inside* an ISO 42001 management system. NIST published a crosswalk between AI RMF and ISO 42001.

## Common ISO 42001 gotchas (Hank's reviewer lens)

1. **No clear role declaration.** Organisations are usually multiple roles (provider/producer/user). Vague scope = audit finding.
2. **AI risk assessment ≠ AI impact assessment.** Two distinct documents. Both required.
3. **Treating ISO 42001 as a checklist without ISO 27001 backbone.** The clauses are management-system clauses: without an existing or parallel ISMS, the AIMS will feel hollow.
4. **No human oversight pattern documented per AI feature.** "We have humans in the loop" is not evidence. Pattern + procedure + record is evidence.
5. **No third-party AI supplier register.** Every LLM provider, image-gen API, voice API needs to be in the supplier register with risk assessment.
6. **Customer-facing AI without transparency notice.** A.8.4 + AI Act Art. 50 both mandate disclosure.
7. **Event logging not retained long enough for incident reconstruction.** Define retention upfront (typically 12 months minimum for AI inputs/outputs).
8. **No process for handling model retirement / replacement.** When you swap Claude 4.5 for Claude 4.6, what's the impact assessment, regression test, customer notification?
