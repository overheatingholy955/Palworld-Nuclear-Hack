# ISO/IEC 27001:2022: Information Security Management System

**Citation:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (current edition, supersedes 2013). Companion implementation guidance in ISO/IEC 27002:2022. Published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).

**Enforcement:** ISO 27001 is a **certification** standard. Accredited certification bodies (BSI, Bureau Veritas, DNV, TÜV, Schellman, A-LIGN, etc.) perform the certification audit. Three-year certificate with annual surveillance audits. No fines; loss of certificate kills enterprise deals + regulated-sector contracts.

---

## Scope: who it applies to

Any organisation, of any size, sector or location, that wants to demonstrate a systematic, risk-based approach to information security. Particularly important for:
- B2B SaaS selling into regulated sectors (finance, healthcare, government)
- Multinational organisations needing a single internationally-recognised standard
- EU government / EU enterprise procurement (often required, where SOC 2 is acceptable in US)
- Critical infrastructure operators (NIS2 alignment)

---

## Structure: Clauses + Annex A

**Clauses 4–10 = mandatory ISMS requirements:**

- **Clause 4: Context of the organisation.** Internal + external issues, interested parties, scope of the ISMS.
- **Clause 5: Leadership.** Top management commitment, policy, roles + responsibilities.
- **Clause 6: Planning.** Risk assessment + risk treatment, security objectives.
- **Clause 7: Support.** Resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information.
- **Clause 8: Operation.** Operational planning + control, risk assessment + treatment execution.
- **Clause 9: Performance evaluation.** Monitoring, internal audit, management review.
- **Clause 10: Improvement.** Nonconformity + corrective action, continual improvement.

This is the **PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) backbone** of every ISO management-system standard.

**Annex A = list of 93 reference controls** (down from 114 in the 2013 edition). Controls are now grouped into 4 themes (previously 14 domains):

| Theme | Controls | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Organisational** | 37 | Policies, roles, threat intel, asset mgmt, access control, supplier mgmt, incident mgmt, BC, compliance |
| **People** | 8 | Screening, terms of employment, awareness, disciplinary, post-employment, remote working, reporting |
| **Physical** | 14 | Secure perimeters, areas, equipment, clear desk, cabling, off-site assets, disposal |
| **Technological** | 34 | Endpoints, access mgmt, authentication, crypto, ops security, vuln mgmt, logging, network security, SDLC, data loss prevention, redundancy |

**New controls in 2022 (vs 2013): 11 introduced:** Threat intelligence (A.5.7), Cloud services use (A.5.23), ICT readiness for business continuity (A.5.30), Physical security monitoring (A.7.4), Configuration mgmt (A.8.9), Information deletion (A.8.10), Data masking (A.8.11), Data leakage prevention (A.8.12), Monitoring activities (A.8.16), Web filtering (A.8.23), Secure coding (A.8.28).

## Statement of Applicability (SoA)

The single most important ISMS document. Lists all 93 Annex A controls with:
- **Inclusion or exclusion**: exclusions must be justified ("not applicable because we have no physical office")
- **Implementation status**: implemented / partial / planned / not applicable
- **Justification** for the decision
- **Reference to the implementing policy/procedure**

The SoA is the first thing the certification auditor reads. Keep it under version control. Treat it as a living document.

## Risk-based approach

ISO 27001 is **not a checklist standard.** Annex A is a *reference set*: you pick controls based on a documented **risk assessment**:

1. **Identify assets + threats + vulnerabilities** (or use a scenario-based or event-based approach; the 2022 edition does not prescribe a method).
2. **Assess risk**: likelihood × impact.
3. **Treat risk**: mitigate (apply controls), transfer (insurance, contract), avoid (stop doing it), accept (document + sign off).
4. **Map treatment to Annex A controls** + supplementary controls from other frameworks if needed.
5. **Document in the SoA.**

**Risk treatment plan (RTP)** + **Risk acceptance record** = the audit evidence trail.

---

## Mandatory documented information

The 2022 edition requires:

- ISMS scope
- Information security policy + topic-specific policies
- Risk assessment process + criteria
- Risk treatment process
- Statement of Applicability
- Risk assessment + treatment results
- Security objectives
- Evidence of competence
- Evidence of monitoring + measurement
- Internal audit programme + results
- Management review results
- Nonconformities + corrective action records

Anything beyond this is good practice but not strictly mandatory.

## Certification lifecycle

1. **Stage 1 audit** (documentation review): auditor reviews policies, SoA, risk assessment. Goal: confirm the ISMS is mature enough for stage 2.
2. **Stage 2 audit** (certification audit, on-site or remote): sampling-based testing of controls, interviews, evidence review. Auditor issues a recommendation.
3. **Certificate issued**: valid for 3 years.
4. **Surveillance audits**: annually, sampling part of the control set each visit.
5. **Recertification**: full audit at the 3-year mark.

**Typical timeline from kickoff to certificate:** 4–9 months for a small org with a competent partner.

## ISO 27001 + AI controls (emerging best practice)

ISO 42001 (AI Management System: see separate brief) is the AI-specific complement. ISO 27001 covers info security; ISO 42001 covers AI governance, ethics, lifecycle. They're designed to integrate. A-LIGN and other auditors offer joint 27001+42001 assessments.

Within ISO 27001 itself, AI-relevant control mapping:
- **A.5.7 Threat intelligence**: track AI-specific threat intel (OWASP LLM Top 10, MITRE ATLAS)
- **A.5.23 Cloud services use**: LLM API providers (Anthropic, OpenAI) are cloud services with all the supplier obligations
- **A.8.11 Data masking**: for PII in prompts + training data
- **A.8.12 Data leakage prevention**: for prompt injection extraction attempts
- **A.8.16 Monitoring activities**: for AI output anomalies + abuse
- **A.8.28 Secure coding**: for prompt-template + RAG pipeline development

## ISO 27001 vs SOC 2: the buyer's view

See `soc2.md` for the comparison table. Short version:
- US enterprise SaaS sales motion → SOC 2 Type II
- EU government / multinational / regulated sectors → ISO 27001
- Both → joint audit (Schellman, A-LIGN and BSI all offer this; the shared evidence makes it cheaper than two separate audits)

## Common ISO 27001 gotchas (Hank's reviewer lens)

1. **SoA marks controls "implemented" without evidence.** Auditor will sample-test: implementation claim without policy + procedure + evidence is a major finding.
2. **Risk register written once, never updated.** Risk assessment is a continuous process per Clause 6 + 8. A stale register kills surveillance audits.
3. **Internal audit done by the same person who owns the controls.** Internal audit must be independent of the controls being audited. For a small org, hire an external internal auditor.
4. **Management review skipped or perfunctory.** Clause 9.3 requires real content: performance, audit results, risk changes, improvement opportunities, decisions. Minutes are evidence.
5. **No evidence of awareness training.** Clause 7.3: awareness is mandatory. Need attendance records + content.
6. **Asset inventory missing or stale.** Foundational: every other control depends on knowing what you're protecting.
7. **Supplier controls weak.** A.5.19–A.5.22: supplier register, security in supplier agreements, monitoring + review, change management. Easy to skip, frequently audited.
